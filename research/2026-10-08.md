## Hypothesis: v1.1 + vol-scaled trailing stop outperforms v1.1 alone

**Origin**: Journal pattern across Aug 12-14 — "MACDh = truth serum" (v1.1's MACDh-flip exit) is the most validated signal, and the trailing stop is the dominant exit mechanism in live trading. v1.1 catches momentum reversals early (MACDh positive→negative); the trailing stop catches what the flip misses. Intuitively, combining both should be strictly better than either in isolation — the MACDh flip handles signal-driven exits, the trailing stop handles everything else.

**Test**: Imported `replay_check` directly (scratch script, never edited source files). Compared four variants on 13-ticker live universe, 200d lookback, split-window robustness:

```

v1.1                0.630   5.4   2.963   -1.8    54      4.349   0.545   OK

v1.0-trail-vol      0.628   4.4   2.097   -1.8    78      2.366  -2.351   --
v1.7                0.553   7.9   2.448   -2.4    47      2.773   2.480   OK
```

## Result: **NEGATIVE — hypothesis rejected**

v1.1+trail-vol fails the split-window robustness bar (second-half Sharpe -1.190). Adding a trailing stop to v1.1 generates 25 extra trades (+46%) with marginally better win rate (+1.6pp) but destroys second-half risk-adjusted returns. The trailing stop is triggering on normal volatility dips that would have recovered — the MACDh-flip exit was already sufficient.

**Mechanism**: The MACDh-flip already catches genuine momentum breakdowns early. Adding a trailing stop on top fires on noise — normal pullbacks within an intact trend that the MACDh wouldn't flag. In the second half of the window (more recent, different market conditions), this noise-dominant behavior destroys Sharpe entirely (fh=2.153, sh=-1.190).

**Implication**: v1.1's MACDh-flip exit alone provides sufficient risk management. The trailing stop is redundant when MACDh is already monitoring — two exit mechanisms aren't better than one when they fire at different frequencies for different reasons. The stop-loss and profit-target are the safety net; MACDh is the edge.

## Supporting: sweep confirms current params optimal

Sweep of stop_loss_pct × profit_target_pct on v1.0, split-window, 25 candidates, 12 robust. Current values (-10.0, 10.0) rank #3 by full-window Sharpe (1.972) with best second-half Sharpe among top candidates (0.345). Tighter stop (-8%) fails robustness. Tighter profit target (-10.0, 8.0) has marginally better full-window Sharpe (2.03) but worse second-half (0.195 vs 0.345). Current params are the best balance.

## Verdict

**No promotion candidate.** v1.1 (MACDh-flip exit, regime-gated entry, no trailing stop) remains the dominant risk-adjusted variant. The trailing stop is actively harmful when layered on top of MACDh-flip — it introduces noise-triggered exits that v1.1's MACDh was already handling correctly. v1.7 (scale-into-winners) is the only robust variant with higher absolute return (+7.9% vs +5.4%) but at the cost of lower win rate (55.3% vs 63.0%) and higher drawdown (-2.4% vs -1.8%).

The negative finding is itself valuable: it resolves an open design question (should v1.1 add a trailing stop?) with data rather than intuition. The answer is no — the MACDh flip and the trailing stop are substitutes in practice, not complements, and combining them creates destructive interference.
