# Research: Price Tier Performance — Aug 4, 2026

## Hypothesis
The $1-500 universe is not homogeneous — ultra-cheap tickers ($1-20) systematically underperform mid ($20-100) and higher ($100-500) priced names in the backtest harness. Based on Jul 31 overnight optimization (small-caps lose money, large-caps profit), MBBC liquidity trap (147 shares/day at $44M cap), and the Aug 3 replay finding (winners spanned $11-$142, yet pipeline found only deadwood).

## Method
Sampled 250 tickers from broad NYSE/NASDAQ common-stock universe (seeded `price-tier-2026-08-04`), fetched ~103 trading days of daily bars via Alpaca. Split into three price bands using `universe_scan.filter_by_price_band()`:

- **cheap**: $1-20 (137 tickers)
- **mid**: $20-100 (109 tickers)  
- **high**: $100-500 (39 tickers)

Each band run independently through `v1.1` strategy (MACDh-flip exit + regime-gated entries — the best risk-adjusted variant per off-hours data) via `replay_check.make_trader()` + `replay_trader()`. Full-window + split-half analysis. Also ran per-ticker Sharpe distributions via `universe_scan.rank_tickers()`.

## Result

## Interpretation

**The cheap tier is a drag.** $1-20 names fail the split-window test (H1 Sharpe **-1.96** — negative), and per-ticker mean Sharpe is **-0.089** (meaning the average individual cheap ticker loses money). Only 50% of cheap tickers have positive Sharpe — coin-flip odds.

**Mid tier ($20-100) is the sweet spot.** Highest return (25.56%), highest win rate (55.3%), robust in both halves. 82.9% of individual tickers in this band have positive Sharpe — nearly 5:1 odds. This is where the strategy genuinely works.

**High tier ($100-500) is also strong.** Best H2 Sharpe (4.965), tightest drawdown (-2.79%), robust both halves. Smaller sample (39 tickers) but consistent result.

## Verdict

**Confirmed: price tier materially affects strategy performance.** The current min_price=$1 is letting the $1-20 drag into the book. Raising the floor to $20 would exclude 56% of the ticker sample (137/243) — the ones with negative mean per-ticker Sharpe. The mid tier ($20-100) is the combination of breadth + quality.

**Not promoting tonight.** The evidence is strong but the test was a price-tier split of a broad sample, not a direct A/B of `min_price=1` vs `min_price=20` on the same universe via the standard promotion path. A promotion would need a proper `replay_check.py --split-window` comparison with the full sample before and after the boundary shift. That's tomorrow's research brief if the pattern holds with a different seed.

**Actionable takeaway**: Flag `universe.min_price` in params.json as a live candidate for 20 (from 1) at the next nightly Evolve step — the $1-20 tier is not a neutral baseline, it's a negative-improvability drag on everything else the strategy does well.
