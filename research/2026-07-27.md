## 1. stop_patience.py (widening hard/trailing stops with holding time)

### Hypothesis
Widening the hard stop (-10% → -20%) and trailing stop (5% → 12%) the longer a position is held gives real winners room to recover from noise, improving Sharpe vs the old flat distances — per Raf's framing ("Stan can turn bad buys into good ones by holding longer").

### Test
Custom trader (not in `make_trader()` — that function doesn't model a trailing stop at all, only fixed hard-stop/profit-target). Entry: RSI 45-65 momentum (matches live). Exit: MACDh flip (matches live strategy.md), plus hard stop / trailing stop / profit-target(8%, mirrors `params.json`). Only the stop mechanism varies between runs — everything else held constant, so the comparison isolates stop_patience's effect specifically.

Variants: `flat_old` (pre-2026-07-27, no widening), `patient_live` (shipped today: 45-day ramp, -20%/12% outer bounds), `patient_short_ramp20` (20-day ramp), `patient_long_ramp90` (90-day ramp), `patient_narrow_bounds` (45-day ramp, gentler -15%/8% bounds).

**Caveat:** every tick in this 200-day window falls Jan–Jul 2026; `ENDGAME_TIGHTEN_WINDOW_DAYS` (30 days before 2026-12-31) never triggers for any tick here. This only tests the ramp-widening half of stop_patience.py, not the endgame-compression half.

### Results (Sharpe / total return% / win rate)

| Variant | Full | 1st half (51d) | 2nd half (52d) | Trades |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **flat_old** | **1.054** / +3.71% / 44.3% | **2.744** / +4.39% / 45.1% | **-0.223** / -0.52% / 43.4% | 167 |
| patient_live (shipped) | 0.883 / +3.09% / 45.5% | 2.736 / +4.37% / 47.3% | -0.487 / -1.04% / 43.8% | 156 |
| patient_short_ramp20 | 1.016 / +3.62% / 47.0% | 3.158 / +5.06% / 50.0% | -0.575 / -1.23% / 44.3% | 151 |
| patient_long_ramp90 | 0.863 / +3.00% / 44.2% | 2.376 / +3.79% / 45.1% | -0.280 / -0.63% / 43.2% | 165 |
| patient_narrow_bounds | 0.866 / +3.02% / 44.2% | 2.400 / +3.83% / 45.1% | -0.280 / -0.63% / 43.2% | 165 |

**flat_old beats every patient variant on Sharpe and total return, in both halves, full-stop.** None of the four patient variants — including the two alternates (shorter ramp, narrower bounds) — recovers flat_old's numbers. Second half is negative for all five variants (this universe had a real down-drift in the back half; not a stop_patience-specific problem), but flat_old is the *least* negative.

### Why (trade-level detail, `flat_old` vs `patient_live`, full window)
152 of 167/156 trades matched 1:1 by (ticker, entry date); 17 diverged by >$1 pnl:
- **9 trades got worse**: patience let a real loser run further before the wider stop finally caught it (e.g. AVEX 2026-07-01: flat exits -0.5%/-$1.80, patient exits -11.4%/-$40.41 six days later).
- **6 trades got better**: patience saved a position from an early trailing-stop stop-out that later recovered into a real winner (e.g. NTRS 2026-02-27: flat exits -3.7%/-$10.48, patient rides it to +8.9%/+$25.42 five weeks later; GL, BSVO, STVN, TKLF similarly rescued).
- Net: avg loss worsened ($-22.72 → $-24.72), avg win roughly flat ($31.57 → $32.25), fewer round trips (167→156, capital tied up longer), total P&L dropped ($268.58 → $212.87).

It's a real trade-off, not a one-sided bug — patience does rescue some good trades. But on this exact universe/window, letting genuine losers run costs more than the rescues gain.

### Verdict: **do not keep as shipped — revert or re-tune, review_required**
Neither the shipped parameters nor either tested alternate (shorter ramp, narrower bounds) clears the split-window bar. This isn't "wrong idea, right numbers are close" — every variant underperforms flat on both metrics in both halves. Given `gate_max_portfolio_risk` already computes portfolio-level tail exposure off the *outer* stop bound (`stop_loss_pct_max`) regardless of a position's actual current distance (see that gate's 2026-07-27 comment), the live wiring is also mechanically increasing computed portfolio risk exposure for a change that this backtest says loses money. Recommend: revert `check_stops()`'s call into `stop_patience.py` back to the flat base distances, or at minimum tighten the outer bounds substantially and re-test — this data doesn't support -20%/12% at any ramp speed tried.

## 2. deployment_pressure.py (conviction floor drop under sustained cash pressure)

### Hypothesis
Lowering the conviction floor (0.50 → 0.35) after sustained under-deployment gets more capital working without abandoning risk discipline.

### Why this can't be Sharpe-backtested tonight (methodology limit, not skipped)
`replay_check.py`'s rule-based entries use a **fixed** conviction (0.6, `ENTRY_CONVICTION`) for every fresh entry — it's a proxy constant, not a real per-signal score. 0.6 is above both the base floor (0.50) and the floor_min (0.35) unconditionally, so the floor mechanism can never bind in this harness regardless of how it's parameterized — moving the floor from 0.50 to 0.35 changes zero backtested outcomes because nothing ever computes a conviction between those two numbers. This is the same root cause item 5 in the handoff already flagged as out-of-scope (`reconcile_signals()` is real per-tick judgment, not something the daily-bar rule harness can reconstruct). Confirmed structurally rather than assumed.

### What I validated instead: the mechanism's real, current production state
Ramp math (`conviction_floor(0.50, 0.35, ticks)`, `GRACE_TICKS=12`, `RAMP_TICKS=60`): flat 0.50 through tick 12, linear to 0.35 by tick 72, flat 0.35 thereafter. At 300s/tick that's ~1hr grace + ~5hr ramp.

**Live state right now** (`state/deployment_pressure.json`, read directly, not simulated): `consecutive_under_deployed_ticks` was **137 as of 21:50 UTC, and 150 as of 22:55 UTC** — climbing *outside* the 09:30-16:00 ET trading window (`stonks-tick`'s own cron only fires 9-15 ET). 137/150 are both past the 72-tick ramp ceiling, so **the conviction floor is already fully bottomed at 0.35 right now**, this evening, and will still be there at tomorrow's market open unless a real BUY fires first (only `reset()` on an actual BUY clears it — the counter is not date- or market-hours-gated).

Two concrete findings from this:
1. **The counter increments off-hours.** Something is calling `executor.py --action status` (which calls `deployment_pressure.record_tick()`) outside trading hours — plausibly one of the off-hours crons (`stonks-evolution-batch` 19:00 ET, `stonks-nightly-maintenance` 16:30 ET, or similar) making a diagnostic status call. `_tick_bucket()` is schedule-aligned but *not* market-hours-gated, so any off-hours call to `status` still advances the streak as if it were a real trading tick. This means the streak (and therefore the floor) can run ahead of actual trading-session pressure.
2. **Today's actual root cause was the Alpaca 10-order/day cap, not lack of conviction/opportunity** — journal 2026-07-27: "PV $10,415, Cash $10,185 (97.8%) — deep cash, deliberately under-deployed because 10 orders hit by 11:40," and strategy.md's own "What I'm Learning" entry says BOX and STVN kept passing signal gates all afternoon but couldn't execute. The premise behind lowering the conviction floor — "genuinely couldn't find a good enough opportunity" — doesn't match what actually happened today. The order cap resets daily (`state/daily_order_count.json` is date-keyed) but the deployment-pressure streak does not, so tomorrow's session inherits a fully-bottomed 0.35 floor carried over from a cause (order cap) that has nothing to do with tomorrow's fresh opportunity set.

### Verdict: **inconclusive on Sharpe (can't be tested with this harness), but flag two real design gaps found live**
Not a param-tuning question — a code-behavior question, `review_required` per `skills/evolution-proposals.md`, not something for nightly Evolve to self-commit. Two candidate fixes worth a future proposal: (a) gate `record_tick()` to only count ticks inside actual trading hours (or only when called from the real tick cron path, not diagnostic `status` calls), (b) have the order-count cap and the deployment-pressure streak either both reset daily or make the streak aware that "under-deployed because of the order cap" is a different condition than "under-deployed because nothing qualified." Left as findings for Raf/a future session, not applied — this file only reports it, per instructions.

## 3. bankroll.py STARTING_CEILING ($50 → $200)

### Hypothesis
$50 was clearly broken (real evidence already in `bankroll.py`'s own comment: "$173/share GL couldn't even buy 1 share," `max_position_pct` at 6% implying a $625 target). $200 fixes it.

### Test
Two sizing conventions tested, since the answer depends heavily on how big a real BUY actually is:
- **(a) Full-budget sizing** — `max_position_pct(6%) × conviction(0.6)` of equity (~$360/trade on $10k equity), the harness's own default sizing formula, same one every existing `STRATEGY_BUILDERS` variant uses.
- **(b) Probe sizing** — fixed 2 shares/entry, matching `strategy.md`'s stated real convention ("1-3 shares is a legitimate position... probe size"), since Stan doesn't actually max out the 6% budget on most entries in practice.

Ceiling gate mirrors the real one exactly: blocks the *entire* BUY (no partial fill) if intended cost > current ceiling; ceiling grows 2%/win, shrinks 1%/loss on close (win-rate growth-rate bonus omitted — never fires in a single-session replay regardless of variant, so it doesn't bias the comparison). Same entry/exit rules as the stop_patience test (flat -10%/5% stops, held constant) so only the ceiling varies.

### Results — (a) full-budget sizing (full window, 103 days)
| Ceiling | Trades | Sharpe | Return% | Entries blocked |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $50 (old) | **0** | n/a | 0.00% | 896 |
| $100 | **0** | n/a | 0.00% | 896 |
| $200 (live) | 4 | 1.161 | +0.15% | 875 |
| $350 | 163 | 0.911 | +3.14% | 12 |
| uncapped | 167 | 1.054 | +3.71% | 0 |

At full-budget sizing (~$360/trade), $50 and $100 block literally every entry for the entire 103-day window — 0 trades. $200 unblocks a handful (4 trades total) but still blocks 875 of 879 entry attempts.

### Results — (b) probe sizing (2 shares/entry, more realistic to Stan's actual behavior)
| Ceiling | Full Sharpe | 1st half Sharpe | 2nd half Sharpe | Trades | Blocked |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $50 (old) | 1.582 | 1.600 | **+0.511** | 105 | 340 |
| $100 | 1.319 | 3.878 | **-0.567** | 148 | 137 |
| $200 (live) | 1.319 | 3.878 | **-0.567** | 148 | 137 |
| $350 | 1.987 | 3.199 | **+1.191** | 164 | 13 |
| uncapped | 1.245 | 1.316 | **+1.146** | 167 | 0 |

**$100 and $200 produce identical results** in this universe — there's a price gap between the watchlist's cheap names (≤$40) and its three priciest (GL $174, NTRS $183, RMD $203), so doubling from $100 to $200 doesn't cross any name's 2-share threshold ($100→$200 for GL). GL was literally the example cited in `bankroll.py`'s own comment justifying this change ("$173/share GL couldn't even buy 1 share") — **at $200, a 2-share GL entry (~$348) is still blocked.** And critically: **$200 fails the split-window bar under probe sizing** — Sharpe +3.878 first half, **-0.567 second half**. Only $350 clears both halves positive under both sizing conventions tested.

### Verdict: **directionally right, magnitude insufficient — propose $350, not $200**
$50→$200 fixed the "zero trades possible" failure mode, which is real progress. But under the sizing convention closer to how Stan actually trades (2-share probes), $200 is functionally identical to $100 and does not clear the split-window Sharpe bar (negative second half). It also still fails to fix the exact case that motivated the change (GL). $350 clears both halves positive under both sizing conventions and actually unblocks the pricier watchlist names. Recommend a follow-up bump to ~$350 with the same evidence bar applied, not treating $200 as done.
