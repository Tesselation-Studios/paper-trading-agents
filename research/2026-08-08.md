# Research — Saturday August 8, 2026

## Hypothesis

**Combining v1.1's regime-gated entries + MACDh-flip exits with v1.0-trail-vol's volatility-scaled trailing stop produces better risk-adjusted returns than either variant alone.**

Source: off_hours/2026-08-08.md flagged v1.0-trail-vol (Sharpe 3.50) dramatically outperforming live v1.7 (Sharpe 1.31). But that was aggregate-only — no split-window. Journal 2026-08-07's nightly replay practice identified regime-gating and MACDh-flip as the dominant winning signals. The obvious next question: do regime-gating + vol-scaled trail complement each other, or does one subsume the other?

## Test

Scratch Python script (not committed — one-off, deleted after run) monkeypatched `STRATEGY_BUILDERS` in-memory with a `v1.1-trail-vol` variant: `make_trader(frames, "v1.1", vol_scaled_trail=True)`. Ran split-window against `v1.1` and `v1.0-trail-vol` on 36 live-universe tickers, 200-day lookback, 105 trading days.

### Split-window detail

- **v1.1-trail-vol first half**: Sharpe 2.803, return +5.2%, max DD -2.19% — marginally better first-half Sharpe than v1.1 (2.803 > 2.726). The trail added 0.077 Sharpe points to the first half.
- **v1.1-trail-vol second half**: Sharpe 3.349, return +6.7%, max DD -1.69% — clearly WORSE than v1.1 alone (3.349 < 4.378). The trail cost 1.029 Sharpe points in the second half.
- **v1.0-trail-vol fails promotion bar**: First half Sharpe 1.474, max DD -5.18%. The aggregate 2.508 is a second-half illusion — the off-hours didn't have split-window data.

## Verdict: **NEGATIVE**

The v1.1-trail-vol combo does NOT beat v1.1 alone. Adding vol-scaled trailing stops to a strategy that already has regime-gated entries and MACDh-flip exits adds marginal first-half consistency (Sharpe +0.077) while destroying second-half performance (Sharpe -1.029). Net negative (aggregate Sharpe 3.076 < 3.774, return 11.2% < 14.5%).

**Why**: Regime-gating and MACDh-flip exits already handle the two problems vol-scaled trails are supposed to solve — regime-gating keeps you out of bad markets, MACDh-flip gets you out before trailing stops would even trigger. Adding a third exit mechanism on top creates premature exits in strong trends (the second half) without meaningfully improving the first half. The trail is redundant when the other two filters are present.

- **v1.1 alone is the sweet spot**: Both halves Sharpe 2.7+, highest aggregate Sharpe (3.774), highest win rate (48.3%), lowest drawdown (-2.21%). No trail. No scale-in. Just regime-gating + MACDh-flip + fixed stops.
