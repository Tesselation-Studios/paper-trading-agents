# Scale-Into-Winners Isolation Test — 2026-07-30

**Hypothesis**: Scale-into-winners (v1.7 mechanic) degrades risk-adjusted returns on v1.0's clean entry rules — the mechanic itself, not just v1.1's complexity, is the primary cause of Sharpe degradation.

**Observation grounding**: 3 journals (Jul 28-30) consistently show v1.0 (simple RSI, fixed stop/target) dominating v1.7 on Sharpe (3.75 vs 2.76 in off-hours replay). But that replay compared bundles — nobody ever isolated which v1.7 component caused the degradation. Meanwhile, BOX (+$27, 8 scale-ins, strong MACDh) was a scale-in success and OLP (-5.38%, weak MACDh +0.2554) was a forced entry that failed in 40 minutes. The asymmetry suggests scale-in quality depends on entry quality.

## Method

Parameterized in-memory test only — no file edits. Added scale-into-winners to v1.0 at three multiples (1.5x, 2.0x, 3.0x) and compared against v1.0 baseline and v1.7 (v1.1 rules + scale-in at 3.0x). Split-window robustness check (Sharpe positive in both halves independently). 31-ticker live universe, 200d lookback.

```python
variants = {
    'v1.0':          lambda f: make_trader(f, 'v1.0'),
    'v1.0-scale-1.5': lambda f: make_trader(f, 'v1.0', scale_into_winners=True, scale_in_max_multiple=1.5),
    'v1.0-scale-2.0': lambda f: make_trader(f, 'v1.0', scale_into_winners=True, scale_in_max_multiple=2.0),
    'v1.0-scale-3.0': lambda f: make_trader(f, 'v1.0', scale_into_winners=True, scale_in_max_multiple=3.0),
    'v1.7':          lambda f: make_trader(f, 'v1.1', scale_into_winners=True),
}
```

## Analysis

**The monotonic degradation is clear**: adding scale-in to v1.0 degrades Sharpe in lockstep with the scale-in multiple — 2.945 → 2.582 → 2.364 → 2.304. Drawdowns follow the same staircase: -2.79% → -6.62% → -6.16% → -7.72%. More aggressive scale-in = worse risk-adjusted returns, period.

**But v1.7 breaks the pattern**: v1.7 (v1.1 rules + scale-in at 3.0x) scores 3.008 Sharpe, beating even v1.0 baseline (2.945). This is the exact opposite of what the off-hours replay (12-ticker subset) showed. The 31-ticker live universe reveals that v1.1's entry filters (regime gates, MACDh confirmation) are actually PROTECTIVE — they filter out the weak-RSI entries that v1.0 would scale into and lose on.

**The mechanism**: v1.0 enters purely on RSI band. When it catches a genuine winner, the trade is clean (high Sharpe, low DD). When it catches a stinker and scale-in compounds it at +3% during a dead-cat bounce before reversal, the loss is 2-3x worse than it would have been without scale-in. v1.1's filters (regime gate, MACDh check) screen out many of these stinkers before entry, so scale-in is applied to fewer bad trades.

**This directly validates the BOX/OLP pattern from the live journal**: BOX (strong MACDh throughout) + scale-in = excellent campaign. OLP (weak MACDh +0.2554) + forced entry = immediate failure. The asymmetry is real and quantifiable.

## Verdict

**INCONCLUSIVE — hypothesis partially refuted, finding is more nuanced than expected.**

The original hypothesis ("scale-into-winners degrades Sharpe regardless of entry rules") is wrong. Scale-in degrades Sharpe when combined with loose entry rules (v1.0), but actually IMPROVES Sharpe when combined with tighter entry filters (v1.1 → v1.7 beats v1.0 on this universe). The entry filters and scale-in are a package — one without the other is worse than either alone.

**Does not clear the promotion bar**: While v1.7 has the highest full-window Sharpe (3.008 > v1.0's 2.945), the split-window shows v1.0 dominating the second half (4.822 vs 3.138). The v1.7 advantage might be universe-specific or time-period-specific. No strategy change warranted from this test alone.

**Actionable insight**: The protective role of v1.1's entry filters (regime gate, MACDh confirmation) is empirically validated. Any future proposal to simplify entry rules should preserve or strengthen this gating, not remove it. The MACDh signal that journals call "truth serum" is doing exactly what it claims to — preventing scale-in from compounding bad entries.

**For future research**: Test v1.7 at gentler scale-in multiples (1.5x, 2.0x) — the v1.1 filters + gentler scale-in might recover v1.0's drawdown profile while keeping the return advantage. v1.7-gentle exists in STRATEGY_BUILDERS but wasn't isolated against v1.0-scale-gentle.
