## Four-day experiment synthesis: peak-entry-discipline

### Results summary

| Day | Date | Market | Control | Treatment | Δ | Rule triggered | Changed |
|-----|------|--------|---------|-----------|-----|---------------|---------|
| 1 | 2026-06-01 | Choppy | -$4.30 | -$1.56 | +$2.74 | AMD (11:15) | 1 |
| 2 | 2026-07-29 | Choppy | -$7.80 | -$7.80 | $0.00 | SOFI, DJT | 0 |
| 3 | 2026-07-30 | Momentum | +$3.63 | +$3.63 | $0.00 | SOFI, MARA, KEEL | 0 |
| 4 | 2026-07-24 | Choppy | -$15.30 | -$15.30 | $0.00 | DXCM, DJT | 0 |

**Total**: 1 decision changed across 4 days. Net PnL improvement: +$2.74 on $40K starting capital. Zero false negatives (the rule never blocked a trade that would have won).

### Key findings

1. **The rule works when it fires, but it rarely fires.** It triggered 7 times across 4 days but only changed 1 decision (day 1 AMD timing). The other 6 triggers were redundant — natural judgment already skipped those entries. On the 1 day it did change something, it saved $2.74.

2. **The rule's benefit is asymmetric and positive.** Day 1's $2.74 saving is real. Day 3's SOFI would have been a $0.46 win if entered (the only false-negative risk), but was passed naturally by both arms. The choppy-day savings ($6.01 on day 1 AMD peak entry) dwarf the momentum-day opportunity cost ($0.46 on day 3 SOFI).

3. **The RSI>65 threshold is a cliff, not a slope.** Day 3 NVDA at RSI 64.8 passed through while a virtually identical setup at 65.1 would have been blocked. The 0.3-point difference is noise. A softened version — e.g., "RSI>63: size down; RSI>65: require confirmation; RSI>68: skip" — would be more principled.

4. **Exit discipline is the bigger unsolved problem.** Across all 4 days, the largest single losses came from holding winners through end-of-day market fades, not from bad entries:
   - Day 1 AMD: entry timing (rule helped)
   - Day 2 AMD: entered $437.50, peaked $450.52 (+3.0%), closed $429.16 — lost $8.34 on exit
   - Day 3 NVDA: entered $196.13, faded to EOD $195.04 — small loss but avoidable
   - Day 4 AMD: entered $538.02, closed $522.03 — lost $15.99 when SPY faded
   
   A complementary exit-discipline rule ("if position is green >2% intraday and the last hour shows declining SPY MACDh/RSI, take profits early") would likely have saved more than this entry rule ever could.

5. **The rule's real value: insurance against the 1-in-4 judgment failure.** The rule was redundant 3/4 days — but on the 1 day it wasn't (day 1), it saved meaningful money. It's like a circuit breaker: you hope you never need it, but you're glad it's there when you do.

### Recommendation for strategy.md promotion

**Do not promote as a hard params.json rule.** The evidence base (1 decision changed, 4 days) is too thin for a mechanical gate. But the underlying principle — "RSI>65 + >1% price run on a fresh MACDh flip is a peaked-pump pattern; require extra confirmation or skip" — is worth adding to strategy.md as a **judgment heuristic**, not a mechanical rule. Something like:

> "On a fresh MACDh flip: if RSI is already hot (>65) and price has run >1% in the flip direction, this is the peaked-pump pattern — either require an additional confirming signal or skip and wait for a pullback. This is an operationalization of 'never chase a peaked pump,' not a mechanical veto. Your call."

This preserves the insight without locking in the binary-threshold problems the experiment exposed. If exit discipline ever gets its own experiment and we combine both, the compound effect might justify something harder.
