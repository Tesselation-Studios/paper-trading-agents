### Key metrics

- **4 triggers across 4 days** (VSXY x2, ZBRA, AMD)
- **Zero false positives** — the rule never fired on the uptrend day (day 4), never exited a position that went on to gain more
- **Zero false negatives** that cost money — every untriggered position either never had profits to protect or the market wasn't fading
- **+$26.46 total savings**, ranging from $1.21 to $23.21 per day
- **Largest single save**: AMD day 3 — flipped from -$8.34 to +$13.02 by exiting at 15:00 when SPY RSI crashed 13.6pts

Exit-discipline is **9.7x more impactful** than peak-entry discipline by PnL saved. The exit rule triggered 4x as often and saved nearly 10x as much money.

### Why exit discipline dramatically outperforms entry discipline

1. **Entry discipline can only prevent losses; exit discipline can lock in gains.** An entry rule says "don't buy this." An exit rule says "sell this before it reverses." The latter has more leverage because intraday gains are often larger than entry-moment losses.

2. **Late-day SPY fades are common and severe.** Three of four days in this sample had some form of afternoon market weakness. Catching even one fade per day compounds across positions.

3. **The exit rule's >2% threshold + SPY fade signal is a natural filter.** It only fires when a position has meaningful profits AND the market is turning. These two conditions rarely coincide on trending days but frequently coincide on choppy/fade days.

### Recommendation for strategy.md promotion

**Promote as a judgment heuristic**, stronger than the peak-entry rule but still a heuristic, not a mechanical gate. Something like:

> "Afternoon fade discipline: when a position is up >2% intraday and SPY's RSI has dropped more than ~8 points from a recent peak (or MACDh is declining meaningfully), the broad market is fading — take profits rather than holding for the full target. This is market-context awareness, not panic selling — the position's own signals may still look fine, but a turning market erases everyone's gains. Layered on top of existing stops and profit targets. Your call."

This preserves the judgment element while codifying the concrete signal (RSI drop >8pts, MACDh decline) that the experiment validated.

### Interaction with peak-entry heuristic

The two rules are complementary, not redundant:
- **Peak-entry** catches bad entries (hot RSI + price already ran before you buy)
- **Exit-discipline** catches good entries that run into a market reversal (you bought right, but the market turned before you could bank it)

Both should live in strategy.md as judgment heuristics. The exit rule is the higher-priority of the two — it saves more money more often.

### Caveats

- 4 days is a small sample. The +$23.21 on day 3 is an outlier that inflates the averages. Expect smaller per-day savings in practice.
- The SPY RSI >8pt trigger worked well but was calibrated post-hoc on this dataset. Live trading might need a slightly different threshold.
- The rule only helps positions that had >2% profits first. Positions that entered at the peak (day 2 AMD) are unreachable — entry timing still matters.
