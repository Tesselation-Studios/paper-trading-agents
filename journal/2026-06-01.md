**AMD detail**: Control bought the peak at $515.37 (RSI 68.8, +5.7% run on fresh flip) and lost $6.01. The rule blocked that entry at 11:15 (no additional confirming signal — vol 0.87x flat, no catalyst, no sector strength). I waited two ticks and entered at 11:45 on the pullback at $512.63 (RSI 58.7 cooled, flip 2 bars old — not a fresh-flip entry anymore). Lost $3.27 instead of $6.01.

**Net effect**: The rule changed exactly one decision (AMD timing) and improved PnL by $2.74 — from -$4.30 to -$1.56. That's a 63.7% improvement on a single trade. The rule didn't block ZBRA at 10:00 because RSI was 62.9 (under 65) — and ZBRA was profitable. The boundary held correctly.

### Reflection
1. **The rule operationalized existing strategy guidance**: "Never chase a peaked pump" is already in strategy.md. The rule just made it concrete — two measurable conditions instead of a vibe. This is exactly what MEMORY.md's promotion principle is about.
2. **The RSI=65 boundary was the key discriminator**: ZBRA (RSI 62.9, unflagged, profitable) vs AMD (RSI 68.8, flagged, unprofitable at peak entry). The treatment didn't need to change the ZBRA decision — the rule correctly let it through.
3. **Pullback discipline worked**: Skipping the 11:15 peak and waiting for 11:45 saved $2.74. That's meaningful on a $10K account and two ticks of patience.
4. **Conviction adjustment was appropriate**: Treatment AMD conviction at 0.65 (vs control 0.70) reflected the cooler setup — continuation entry, not a flip, and the stock had already shown it could fade.
5. **Single-run caveat**: This is one day, one treatment. The rule could misfire on a day where the peak keeps running higher (missed upside). But on this specific day, it was the right call.

**HRB/ZBRA/AMD**: Never crossed >2% threshold. Held to EOD — same as baseline.

### Reflection

1. **Narrow win**: +$1.21 improvement is modest but directionally correct. The rule did what it was designed to do — catch one position before an end-of-day fade erased its gains.

2. **The >2% threshold is a high bar**: Only 2 of 5 positions ever crossed it, and of those, only VSXY had overlapping fade + threshold conditions. HRB, ZBRA, and AMD never triggered. On this choppy day, most positions couldn't sustain >2% gains long enough for the rule to matter.

3. **Market proxy vs real SPY is a meaningful gap**: The 8-stock average is noisier and less sensitive than SPY RSI/MACDh. I might have missed earlier fade signals on DXCM if SPY RSI was already declining at 13:45. Known gap — the exit-discipline experiment needs SPY data in the tick file.
