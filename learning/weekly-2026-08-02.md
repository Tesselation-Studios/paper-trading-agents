## Pattern #2: RDDT — Peak Entry + Trailing Stop = Instant Catastrophe (NEW)

**Status**: NEW. RDDT entered at 9:54 ($178.04, MACDh +1.21, vol 2.81x, SUSTAINABLE 0.92), stopped at 10:12 ($137.69, -22.66%, -$40.35). Eighteen minutes. Largest single loss in strategy history.

**Analysis**: The entry thesis was textbook — strong MACDh, solid volume, SUSTAINABLE regime, Tech sector open. The failure wasn't the signal — it was the timing. Buying into a momentum spike that immediately reverses gives the trailing stop zero ratchet room. The first bar after entry was already underwater, and the stop had nowhere to go but trigger.

**This isn't a stop calibration problem.** The 7-18% volatility-scaled trailing stop did exactly what it was designed to do — cut losses before they became catastrophic. -22.66% is brutal but the alternative (no stop) could have been -30%+ given the velocity of the move. The failure was entry timing: 9:54 means buying into the first-30-minute volatility window when spreads are widest and price discovery is still settling.

**Corollary**: The trailing stop win/loss ratio now sits at 6W/15L (28.6%) — declining from 6W/14L (30%). RDDT -22.66% drags the average. Excluding it as an entry-timing outlier: 6W/14L (30%). 16 exits total, crossing the 20-exit formal review trigger. Entry quality — specifically entry timing — is the dominant variable, not stop calibration.

**Verdict**: New rule candidate: "No new entries in the first 30 minutes of trading" — or at minimum, a higher bar (wider initial stop, limit orders, waiting for 10:00 settle). Track one more occurrence before hardening into strategy.md. Add to tasks/pending.md.
